## बंद १ — Refrain

<div class="sher">
<div class="hindi">
आज जाने की ज़िद न करो<br/>
यूँ ही पहलू में बैठे रहो
</div>
</div>

<div class="word-meaning">

| Word | Roman | Meaning |
|------|-------|---------|
| आज | aaj | today |
| जाने की | jaane ki | of going, the act of leaving |
| ज़िद | zid | insistence, stubbornness — the specific word for a determined demand |
| न करो | na karo | don't do, do not |
| यूँ ही | yun hi | just like this, simply, without reason or purpose |
| पहलू में | pehlu mein | beside me, at my side (*pehlu* = flank, side — the intimate nearness of sitting close) |
| बैठे रहो | baithe raho | keep sitting, stay seated — the *raho* suffix gives it duration: don't just sit, keep sitting |

</div>

<div class="explanation">

**What Hashmi is saying:** Don't insist on going today. Just stay here beside me, like this.

The opening is the whole poem in two lines. *Zid* is a word charged with a specific quality: it is the insistence of a child, the stubbornness of someone who has made up their mind. The beloved has decided to leave and is being asked — gently, not with argument but with feeling — to give up that insistence just for today. *Yun hi* — just like this, for no particular reason — is the most tender phrase. Not asking for anything elaborate, not asking the beloved to do anything. Just to stay, just as they are, right here beside me.

</div>

## बंद २

<div class="sher">
<div class="hindi">
हाए मर जाएँगे हम तो लुट जाएँगे<br/>
आज जाने की ज़िद न करो
</div>
</div>

<div class="word-meaning">

| Word | Roman | Meaning |
|------|-------|---------|
| हाए | haaye | an exclamation of grief, longing, or helplessness — untranslatable, it carries the full weight of feeling in a single breath |
| मर जाएँगे | mar jaayenge | we will die — hyperbole of love, the sense that the departure will be unbearable |
| लुट जाएँगे | lut jaayenge | we will be plundered, devastated, left with nothing — *lutna* = to be looted, to be ruined |

</div>

<div class="explanation">

**What Hashmi is saying:** Oh — we will die, we will be left with nothing. Don't insist on going today.

The verse is pure feeling with almost no argument — just the cry *haaye*, the hyperbole of *mar jaayenge*, the devastation of *lut jaayenge*. These are not literal claims. They are the language of a love that knows the departure will leave the room emptied of everything worth having. The refrain returns immediately after: the plea is restated, unchanged, because no elaboration is needed. The feeling speaks for itself.

</div>

## बंद ३

<div class="sher">
<div class="hindi">
तुम ही सोचो ज़रा क्यों न रोकें तुम्हें<br/>
जान जाती है जब उठके जाते हो तुम<br/>
तुम को अपनी क़सम जाने जाना<br/>
आज जाने की ज़िद न करो
</div>
</div>

<div class="word-meaning">

| Word | Roman | Meaning |
|------|-------|---------|
| तुम ही सोचो | tum hi socho | you yourself think, consider it yourself — the *hi* places the weight on *you* |
| ज़रा | zara | a little, just — softens the imperative |
| क्यों न रोकें | kyun na roke | why should we not stop you, how could we not hold you back |
| जान जाती है | jaan jaati hai | life departs, the soul leaves — *jaan* = life, soul |
| उठके जाते हो | uthke jaate ho | when you get up and go |
| तुम को अपनी क़सम | tum ko apni qasam | I swear by your own self, I take your own oath — the most intimate form of swearing: invoking the beloved themselves as the sacred thing |
| जाने जाना | jaane jaana | O one who is about to go, O departing one — a vocative of tender address |

</div>

<div class="explanation">

**What Hashmi is saying:** You yourself think — how could we not stop you? Life itself leaves when you get up and go. I swear by you — O one who is leaving — don't insist on going today.

The verse turns from pure feeling to something closer to argument, but it is an argument made entirely of love. *Tum hi socho* — you think about it yourself — appeals to the beloved's own judgment: if you understand what your leaving does to me, how could you insist? *Jaan jaati hai* — life departs — is the precise measure of what the leaving costs. And then the oath: *tum ko apni qasam* — I swear by you yourself. In Urdu, the most binding oath is sworn by the thing most sacred. To swear by the beloved's own self is to say: nothing is more real to me than you, and by that reality I ask you to stay.

</div>

## बंद ४

<div class="sher">
<div class="hindi">
वक़्त की क़ैद में ज़िंदगी है मगर<br/>
चंद घड़ियाँ यही मेरे साथ रहो<br/>
इन हसीं लम्हों को मत जाने दो<br/>
आज जाने की ज़िद न करो
</div>
</div>

<div class="word-meaning">

| Word | Roman | Meaning |
|------|-------|---------|
| वक़्त की क़ैद में | waqt ki qaid mein | in the prison of time, under time's constraint (*qaid* = imprisonment, confinement) |
| ज़िंदगी है | zindagi hai | life is — life exists within this constraint |
| मगर | magar | but, yet |
| चंद घड़ियाँ | chand ghariyan | a few moments, a short while (*ghari* = a moment, also a unit of time) |
| यही | yahi | right here, here only |
| मेरे साथ | mere saath | with me |
| हसीं | haseen | beautiful, lovely |
| लम्हों को | lamhon ko | these moments |
| मत जाने दो | mat jaane do | don't let go, don't allow to pass |

</div>

<div class="explanation">

**What Hashmi is saying:** Yes, life is a prisoner of time — I know you must go. But stay here with me just a little while. Don't let these beautiful moments go.

The final verse makes the concession that gives the poem its honesty: *waqt ki qaid mein zindagi hai* — life is imprisoned in time, time's constraint is real, the departure will eventually happen. The speaker is not deluded about this. But within that acknowledged reality comes the plea: *chand ghariyan* — just a few moments — *yahi mere saath raho* — stay right here with me. The word *haseen* applied to the moments — *in haseen lamhon ko* — is the poem's most open declaration: these moments are beautiful. The beauty is the reason. Don't let them go. And the refrain returns for the last time, carrying everything the poem has said: the feeling, the oath, the concession, the beauty of the moments — all gathered into the same simple plea it began with. Don't insist on going today.

</div>
