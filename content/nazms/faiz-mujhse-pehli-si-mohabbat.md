mujhse pehli si mohabbat mere mehboob na maang<br>
<br>
maine samjha tha ki tu hai to darakhshaan hai hayaat<br>
tera gham hai to gham-e-dahar ka jhagda kya hai<br>
teri soorat se hai aalam mein bahaaron ko sabaat<br>
teri aankhen jo to mujhko mere naseeb ki lau hai<br>
<br>
mujhse pehli si mohabbat mere mehboob na maang<br>
<br>
aur bhi dukh hain zamaane mein mohabbat ke siva<br>
raahaten aur bhi hain wasl ki raahat ke siva<br>
an-ginat sadiyon ke taareek bahimaana tilism<br>
reshm-o-atlas-o-kamkhwaab mein bunwaaye hue<br>
ja-ba-ja bikte hue koocha-o-baazaar mein jism<br>
khaak mein lithde hue khoon mein nahaaye hue<br>
<br>
mujhse pehli si mohabbat mere mehboob na maang<br>
<br>
main ne jo tujhse wafa ki thi tamanna ki thi<br>
dil mein ek roz yahi sau tha yahi aarzu thi<br>
tumhari yaad ke jab zakhm bharaa aane lage<br>
teri ulfat ke siwaa duniya mein aur kya tha<br>
<br>
mujhse pehli si mohabbat mere mehboob na maang<br>
<br>
aaj bhi dil-kashish hai tera khayaal<br>
phir bhi teri wafa ka yakeen aa gaya<br>
lekin ab who dil-faribi ka waqt nahi<br>
woh jo hum dono mein thaa ek junoon<br>
jis mein dobte the ham aur tu<br>
<br>
mujhse pehli si mohabbat mere mehboob na maang

## The Poem

<div class="sher">
<div class="hindi">
मुझसे पहली सी मोहब्बत मेरे महबूब न माँग
</div>
</div>

<div class="word-meaning">

| Word | Roman | Meaning |
|------|-------|---------|
| मुझसे | mujhse | from me |
| पहली सी | pehli si | like the earlier, like the first — *si* softens: "that same kind of" |
| मोहब्बत | mohabbat | love |
| मेरे महबूब | mere mehboob | my beloved |
| न माँग | na maang | do not ask, do not demand |

</div>

<div class="explanation">

**What Faiz is saying:** Do not ask me, my beloved, for the same love as before.

The opening line is the entire nazm compressed into one sentence. It is not a rejection — it is an explanation in advance of what follows. The *pehli si* — that same kind as before — implies that love itself has not ended, but that its form has changed. The beloved is being asked to understand something, not to accept a departure. The line will return as a refrain, each time heavier with the reasons the poem has accumulated.

</div>

## Band 1 — First Verse

<div class="sher">
<div class="hindi">
मैंने समझा था कि तू है तो दरख़्शाँ है हयात<br/>
तेरा ग़म है तो ग़म-ए-दहर का झगड़ा क्या है<br/>
तेरी सूरत से है आलम में बहारों को सबात<br/>
तेरी आँखें जो तो मुझको मेरे नसीब की लौ है
</div>
</div>

<div class="word-meaning">

| Word | Roman | Meaning |
|------|-------|---------|
| मैंने समझा था | maine samjha tha | I had understood, I used to believe |
| दरख़्शाँ | darakhshaan | radiant, luminous, shining (*darakhshan* = Persian: shining) |
| हयात | hayaat | life (*hayat* = Arabic: life) |
| ग़म-ए-दहर | gham-e-dahar | the sorrow of the world, the grief of time (*dahar* = the age, the world, time itself) |
| झगड़ा | jhagda | quarrel, dispute, the question — here: "what concern is it" |
| सूरत | soorat | face, form |
| आलम | aalam | the world, the universe |
| बहारों को | bahaaron ko | to the springs, to all the seasons of flourishing |
| सबात | sabaat | permanence, stability, continuance |
| नसीब | naseeb | fate, destiny |
| लौ | lau | flame, the guiding light |

</div>

<div class="explanation">

**What Faiz is saying:** I had believed — when you exist, life is luminous. When your grief is all I carry, what quarrel do I have with the world's sorrow? Your face is what gives spring its permanence in the world. Your eyes are the flame of my destiny.

The verse reconstructs the earlier love — what it was, what it promised. This is not nostalgia; it is evidence. The beloved was not merely beautiful but cosmologically significant: her presence made life radiant, her face gave seasons their stability. The lover had elevated her to the point where she replaced the world's suffering as a concern. *Gham-e-dahar ka jhagda kya hai* — what dispute do I have with the world's sorrow, as long as I have yours? This is the love the poem is about to step away from.

</div>

## Band 2 — Second Verse

<div class="sher">
<div class="hindi">
और भी दुख हैं ज़माने में मोहब्बत के सिवा<br/>
राहतें और भी हैं वस्ल की राहत के सिवा<br/>
अन-गिनत सदियों के तारीक बहीमाना तिलिस्म<br/>
रेशम-ओ-अटलस-ओ-कमख़्वाब में बुनवाए हुए<br/>
जा-ब-जा बिकते हुए कूचा-ओ-बाज़ार में जिस्म<br/>
ख़ाक में लिथड़े हुए ख़ून में नहाए हुए
</div>
</div>

<div class="word-meaning">

| Word | Roman | Meaning |
|------|-------|---------|
| और भी दुख | aur bhi dukh | there are other sorrows too |
| ज़माने में | zamaane mein | in the world, in this age |
| मोहब्बत के सिवा | mohabbat ke siva | besides love, other than love |
| राहतें | raahaten | comforts, reliefs, joys |
| वस्ल की राहत | wasl ki raahat | the comfort of union, the joy of meeting |
| अन-गिनत | an-ginat | innumerable, countless |
| सदियों के | sadiyon ke | of centuries |
| तारीक | taareek | dark, darkened |
| बहीमाना | bahimaana | brutal, bestial (*baheem* = beast) |
| तिलिस्म | tilism | spell, enchantment, the whole bewitching structure |
| रेशम-ओ-अटलस-ओ-कमख़्वाब | reshm-o-atlas-o-kamkhwaab | silk and satin and brocade — three fabrics of luxury |
| बुनवाए हुए | bunwaaye hue | woven into, stitched inside |
| जा-ब-जा | ja-ba-ja | everywhere, place by place |
| बिकते हुए | bikte hue | being sold, on sale |
| कूचा-ओ-बाज़ार | koocha-o-baazaar | lane and marketplace, the streets |
| जिस्म | jism | bodies |
| ख़ाक में लिथड़े हुए | khaak mein lithde hue | rolled in dust, smeared with dust |
| ख़ून में नहाए हुए | khoon mein nahaaye hue | bathed in blood |

</div>

<div class="explanation">

**What Faiz is saying:** There are other sorrows in the world besides love. There are other comforts besides the comfort of union. The brutal enchantment of countless dark centuries — woven into silk and satin and brocade. Bodies being sold everywhere in lanes and markets. Smeared in dust. Bathed in blood.

This is the turn the poem makes, and it makes it without warning. The verse pivots from the private universe of lover and beloved to the public universe of exploitation and suffering. The *tilism* — the enchantment, the spell — is not of love but of centuries of oppression, dressed in luxury fabrics to disguise what it is. The bodies sold in the marketplace are not metaphorical. The dust and blood are not symbols.

Faiz was a committed socialist and spent time in prison for his politics. This verse is where that enters the poem — not as an argument but as a vision. He has seen something the earlier lover had not: the world behind the beloved, and it is full of bodies.

</div>

## Band 3 — Third Verse

<div class="sher">
<div class="hindi">
मैंने जो तुझसे वफ़ा की थी तमन्ना की थी<br/>
दिल में एक रोज़ यही सौ था यही आरज़ू थी<br/>
तुम्हारी याद के जब ज़ख़्म भरा आने लगे<br/>
तेरी उल्फ़त के सिवा दुनिया में और क्या था
</div>
</div>

<div class="word-meaning">

| Word | Roman | Meaning |
|------|-------|---------|
| वफ़ा की थी | wafa ki thi | I had been faithful, I had kept faith |
| तमन्ना | tamanna | longing, desire, the wish |
| सौ था | sau tha | was the dream (*sau* = sleep, dream in older usage) |
| आरज़ू | aarzu | desire, longing |
| ज़ख़्म भरा आने लगे | zakhm bhara aane lage | when the wound began to heal, when the wound began to fill |
| उल्फ़त | ulfat | love, deep affection |

</div>

<div class="explanation">

**What Faiz is saying:** The faithfulness I gave you, the longing I had — once this was the only dream, the only desire in my heart. When your memory's wound began to heal — what else was there in the world besides your love?

The verse returns to the interior, to what the earlier love actually felt like from inside. The healing wound is a precise image: there was a time when the lover was recovering from some separation, and in that recovery, the beloved's love was everything — the entire world contracted to one feeling. The question *aur kya tha* — what else was there — is genuine. There was nothing else. That is being acknowledged, not mocked.

</div>

## Band 4 — Fourth Verse

<div class="sher">
<div class="hindi">
आज भी दिल-कशिश है तेरा ख़याल<br/>
फिर भी तेरी वफ़ा का यक़ीन आ गया<br/>
लेकिन अब वो दिल-फ़रेबी का वक़्त नहीं<br/>
वो जो हम दोनों में था एक जुनून<br/>
जिस में डूबते थे हम और तू
</div>
</div>

<div class="word-meaning">

| Word | Roman | Meaning |
|------|-------|---------|
| दिल-कशिश | dil-kashish | heart-pulling, compelling, magnetic (*dil* = heart; *kashish* = pull, attraction) |
| ख़याल | khayaal | thought, image in the mind |
| वफ़ा का यक़ीन | wafa ka yakeen | the certainty of your faithfulness |
| दिल-फ़रेबी | dil-faribi | heart-deceiving, self-deception of the heart (*dil* = heart; *fareb* = deception) |
| जुनून | junoon | obsession, madness, the consuming passion |
| डूबते थे | dobte the | we used to drown, we used to sink |

</div>

<div class="explanation">

**What Faiz is saying:** Even today your thought draws at my heart. Even now I feel the certainty of your faithfulness. But this is no longer the time for the heart's self-deception — that obsession that was between us both, in which we both used to drown.

The verse makes the distinction precise. The love has not died — *aaj bhi*, even today, the pull is real, the faith is real. What has ended is *dil-faribi* — the self-deception of the heart, the willingness to let love consume everything else, to drown in the obsession (*junoon*) that once swallowed both of them. The drowning was mutual. It is over not because it failed but because the world's claim on the lover has become too loud to ignore.

</div>
