Marathi has one of the oldest continuous literary traditions in India. From the abhangas of the Varkari saints — Dnyaneshwar, Namdev, Tukaram — to the modern lyric poets of the twentieth century, the language has always carried its feeling at full intensity.

The modern Marathi kavita emerged in the middle of the last century as poets moved away from classical metres toward a freer, more intimate voice. Mangesh Padgaonkar brought the *bhavgeet* — the emotion-song — to its highest point, poems that sat between lyric verse and music, designed to be felt before they were understood. Suresh Bhat carried the ghazal form into Marathi with a naturalness that made it feel native rather than borrowed. Vinda Karandikar pushed the language toward philosophical depths it had not explored in quite that way before.

This section reads these poems one stanza at a time — the original Devanagari, Roman transliteration, word-by-word meanings, and an explanation of what the poet was saying and why it stays with you.
