Hindi poetry in the modern era found its voice in the *Chhayavaad* movement of the 1920s and 30s — a turn toward the inner world, toward nature, toward the textures of personal feeling. What followed was decades of lyric poets who wrote about love, loss, time, and longing with an intimacy the language had not quite attempted before.

Harivansh Rai Bachchan arrived in that tradition and deepened it — his *Madhushala* used the old Sufi metaphor of wine and the tavern to say things about desire and mortality that no amount of plain statement could reach. Gulzar came later, from the world of film, and brought to Hindi verse a quality of compressed observation, a way of catching the exact moment when feeling becomes visible.

This section reads these poems carefully — the original Devanagari, Roman transliteration for those who don't read the script, word-by-word meanings, and an explanation of what the poet was actually doing with the language.
